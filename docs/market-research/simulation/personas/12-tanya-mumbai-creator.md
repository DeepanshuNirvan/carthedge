# Panelist 12: Tanya Bhatia (Mumbai, fitness creator with a merch and accessories brand, drop seller)

## Profile

- **Who:** Tanya Bhatia, 27. Fitness creator in Mumbai (Andheri West flat, shoots in Bandra gyms), 400k Instagram followers. Her merch and supplement-accessory brand (tees, hoodies, shaker bottles, gym straps, bags) is run day to day by her manager Kriti and a small agency. Tanya owns the brand and the face; she does not touch the tools.
- **Business in numbers:** sells in drops. About 1,500 orders in a 3-day drop, then around 200 a month in between. Roughly six drops a year, so about 11,000 orders a year, but about 1,700 in a drop month and about 200 in a quiet one. Average order ₹1,300, so a drop is about ₹19.5 lakh in 3 days. 60% prepaid, 40% COD (about 600 COD orders per drop). Kriti's rough read from the agency report: the first hour of a drop is about 400 orders.
- **Stack today:** Shopify store, Shiprocket for shipping, Interakt for WhatsApp, all set up and run by the agency on a retainer. Instagram comments and DMs are answered by Kriti plus two part-time freelancers during drops.
- **Comment and DM volume:** a drop reel gets 20,000+ comments, most of them "price?", "link?", "size chart?", "restock kab?", "COD hai?". Message requests pile up to a few thousand a day in drop week. She knows people leave when they get no answer, and she hates that a fan can screenshot a rude or wrong reply.
- **Tool and agency spend:** about ₹1.3 to ₹1.5 lakh a month on average across the year (detail in answer 6), not counting Kriti's salary, freight or ad spend.
- **How she thinks:** brand first, numbers second. Cares about looking premium and never embarrassing the brand, because her sponsors judge her on it. Budget is not the constraint; reliability is. One bad drop weekend costs her more than a year of any tool. Speaks fast Mumbai English with "yaar", "babe", "literally", "scene", "bro", and forwards things to Kriti with "check this".
- **Where CartHedge sits for her:** at best an Instagram DM and comment layer beside Shopify. She will not move her store off Shopify, so the real question is whether CartHedge can sit next to it.

## Round 1

### Stage 1. Cold first impression (Instagram sponsored post)

I'm in bed scrolling Reels after a 6 am session and this comes up as an ad. "Every DM answered. Every order closed in the chat." Okay. My first thought is ManyChat with a new coat of paint, the thing half the creators I know use to send "comment LINK and I'll DM you". Then "CartHedge replies to buyers in their language from your real catalog" and "you approve with a tap". That second line tells me who this is for. A one-person seller with 40 DMs a night. Not for someone whose inbox does 3,000 in a day.

Would I tap? Yes, because DMs and comments are literally my biggest leak, and I'd tap on the phone just to look at it. I would not start a trial from that tap. I'd screenshot it and send it to Kriti with "check this, is this legit?" and move on with my day.

What I'd suspect: I've never heard of the name. There's no face, no sponsor-style proof, no brand I know using it. "In their language" fine, but a bot talking to my fans under my name is the scariest sentence in the ad. If it says something dumb about a supplement or body stuff, that's a screenshot on a gossip page, not a support ticket. "No card" is normal.

What I'd need next: who is behind it, which creators or brands with real volume use it, whether it works with Shopify (I'm not rebuilding my store), and what happens when 3,000 DMs land in one hour.

### Stage 2. Looking around

I wouldn't do this myself, Kriti would. What I'd ask her to check, and what I looked at to be able to talk to her:

- **Reviews and founder:** I'd ask Kriti to look for the founder, any creator-brand customers, and reviews. I did not run that search here, so I can't tell you what's out there. For something that talks to my fans, "no proof" is a no until she finds some.
- **What my agency already pays for DM and WhatsApp (Interakt):** one comparison page lists Growth around ₹2,799 a month and Advanced around ₹3,799 a month, plus message charges of roughly ₹0.95 for marketing messages, and says a markup of 12 to 15 percent sits on top of the conversation charges. Source: [waaru.app Interakt pricing](https://www.waaru.app/interakt-pricing). Prices vary by blog, so I'd have Kriti confirm from our actual invoice.
- **Meta's WhatsApp charges for a drop broadcast:** one guide quotes ₹0.8631 per marketing message before 18% GST, which is about ₹1.02 with GST, and says BSPs add their own markup. It also says service replies start being charged from 1 October 2026 after the first 1,000 free per number. Source: [MyOperator guide to WhatsApp API pricing India 2026](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026). A blast to my 45k opted-in list is therefore about ₹46,000 before any BSP markup. I'd want to know CartHedge's markup, because that's real money to me.
- **ManyChat (what creators use for comment-to-DM):** Pro is $39 a month, or $29 annually, for 2,500 contacts, then about $0.05 per extra contact, and it prices by active contacts. Source: [Blotato on ManyChat pricing](https://www.blotato.com/blog/manychat-pricing). At my size a single viral reel can add tens of thousands of contacts, so ManyChat's contact pricing is a known worry for creators, and that's the gap CartHedge could fill if it prices by orders.

I did three lookups. Nothing else I'd do as Tanya.

### Stage 3. What I took away from the product

Read the product definition and the website copy (or Kriti's summary of it, in my voice). My honest read of live versus coming:

- **Live and relevant to me:** Instagram DM assistant that replies in the buyer's language from a catalog, a practice chat to test replies before anything goes out, tone and language settings, handover rules (complaints, bargaining, bulk, orders above an amount), auto-reply on/off per chat, order links with QR and click and order counts, buyer pays into my own Razorpay, COD confirmation with an optional token, returns and exchanges, GST invoices, customer ledger, insights, alerts to WhatsApp.
- **Not live yet, and it's the part I care most about:** comment-to-DM, WhatsApp auto-replies on my own number, WhatsApp broadcasts, story and photo matching, live selling.
- **What worries me from the copy:** the whole product is written around a boutique owner approving orders one by one. There is no Shopify sync, no mention of Shiprocket, no custom domain (links look like `carthedge.in/o/CODE` and `/s/<store>`), no team roles beyond the seller login, and no proof of how it behaves with 400 orders in the first hour. The only trial is 15 days, and my next drop might be 6 weeks away.
- **What surprised me in a good way:** the "never invents a price or date" rule, handover with an alert, and that the buyer's money goes to my Razorpay. Also every link counts clicks and orders, which is half of what I'd show a sponsor.

### Stage 4. The interview

**1. In your words, what does it do? What confused you?**

It's an AI that answers DMs for you from your catalog, takes the order in the chat, shows the buyer a summary, and puts it on an order board. It also gives you a small store and payment link, COD confirmation to cut returns, invoices and reports. Think "ManyChat plus a mini Shopify plus an order desk". What confused me: is it replacing my Shopify or sitting next to it? The site says it replaces "the store builder", which for me is a red flag, not a feature. And "closes the order in the chat" confused me because in my drops the order mostly happens on the store link. My DM job is send the right link, answer size and COD questions, and keep the junk out. I don't need an AI to type an address into a chat for 1,500 people.

**2. Top 3 things that excite you, ranked, and why for your business**

1. Comment-to-DM, once it ships. Twenty thousand "link?" comments on a drop reel and today a human replies to maybe 300. If it DMs the link, the size chart and the COD info within seconds, that is the single biggest leak closed. Honest note: it isn't live, so this is excitement about the roadmap, not the product.
2. The control panel for the assistant: practice chat, tone, handover rules, a kill switch for auto-reply. For my brand, "I can see exactly what it will say before a fan does" is what makes it even thinkable.
3. COD confirmation and the buyer money going straight to my own Razorpay. 40% of my drop orders are COD, so about 600 per drop. If RTO drops by even 5 points, that's real money, and I don't love a platform sitting on my cash.

**3. Top 3 doubts, fears or deal-breakers**

1. Brand risk in public. A wrong or weird AI reply to a fan on a supplement-accessory brand becomes a screenshot and a sponsor call. "You approve with a tap" works for 40 DMs, not 3,000 in one hour, so the real design question is what it does when nobody can approve. Deal-breaker unless I can lock banned topics, health and body claims, sponsor-competitor mentions, and review transcripts.
2. Drop-scale reliability. 1,500 orders in 3 days, about 400 in the first hour. Shopify has taken my drops for years. Show me a load test, uptime history, a status page, and a human on call during the drop window. If it dies at hour one, I lose ₹5 lakh of sales and my fans' trust, not ₹2,000.
3. It isn't white-label and it doesn't plug into my stack. `carthedge.in` showing in my buyer chats and order pages is a no. No Shopify sync means two sources of stock, which means oversells, which means angry fans. Also account risk: if Meta ever flags automation on my main account, that account is my income. I need to hear exactly how it uses the official API and what happens at rate limits.

Smaller ones: unknown company and founder, who owns the buyer data and my fan list, and my agency probably hating this.

**4. Features missing that you would need**

- Comment-to-DM live, keyword triggers ("LINK", "PRICE", "SIZE"), with a public reply under the comment and sensible rate caps: **must have before I pay.**
- Custom domain and white-label for links, order pages and tracking (no CartHedge name in front of my fans): **must have before I pay.**
- Proof of drop capacity: load-test numbers, an uptime commitment, a status page, drop-week human support: **must have before I pay.**
- Shopify sync for catalog, stock and orders, plus a Shiprocket link, because I'm keeping both: **must have before I pay.**
- Brand guardrails: banned topics and words, transcript review, first-N-replies approval mode, instant kill switch, a spike mode that sends the link instead of chatting: **must have before I pay.**
- Seats with roles for Kriti, the agency and the freelancers, plus an action log: **must have before I pay.**
- Own WhatsApp number automation and official-API broadcasts to opted-in fans with segments: **nice later** (Interakt does it today).
- Sponsor-ready analytics: reel-by-reel or link-by-link orders and revenue, city split, AOV, repeat rate, one shareable read-only view or branded PDF: **nice later**, but the link counts are a good start.
- Drop mode: countdown page, "notify me" list, per-buyer quantity limits, bot and scalper protection: **nice later**, only relevant if I ever move the store.
- Fan codes and affiliate links for creator friends and sponsors: **nice later.**
- Story-reply matching ("ye wala hoodie"): **nice later.**

**5. Features you would never use**

Reseller price tier, custom made-to-order links, walk-in manual orders, the "tum" voice setting (my brand talks "aap" or plain English), and the website's bulk-import-for-boutiques story. Live selling too, since I don't sell live.

**6. What you spend today, and how you like to pay**

Rough monthly average across the year, from what Kriti shows me:

- Agency retainer for the store, drops, ads management and WhatsApp ops: about ₹90,000
- Shopify plan and apps: about ₹18,000
- Interakt subscription plus Meta message charges, averaged over six drops a year (about 45k messages a drop at roughly ₹1.1 all in is about ₹50,000 a drop): about ₹28,000
- Two freelancers on DMs, mostly in drop weeks, averaged: about ₹20,000

That's about ₹1.55 lakh a month, call it ₹1.3 to ₹1.5 lakh in a normal year. Payment: I don't pay tools myself. The brand pays by company card or UPI autopay for monthly and by bank transfer against a GST invoice for anything yearly, because Mehta ji (our CA) wants the GST invoice in the brand's name. Yearly upfront is fine once it has proved itself on a real drop.

**7. Price sensitivity for the plan you'd need (₹/month, ex-GST unless I say otherwise)**

I'd be on the top plan with comment-to-DM, my own WhatsApp number, seats and drop support.

- (a) So cheap I'd doubt it: under about ₹1,500 a month. A tool that talks to 400k people for the price of a Netflix and a half doesn't feel like it can survive a drop.
- (b) A bargain: ₹4,000 to ₹6,000 a month.
- (c) Getting expensive but I'd still pay: ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month, or about ₹25,000 for a drop month if a human is on call.
- (d) Too expensive: ₹20,000+ a month flat for a DM layer, or any percentage of sales.

**8. React to the current price list**

Pro at ₹1,999 is the only plan that fits me, since the assistant is Pro only. Its 2,000-order quota covers a 1,500 plus 200 drop month with room, so I'd never hit the ₹2 per-order fee. Honestly the number scares me more than it excites me: for what my agency charges, a ₹1,999 tool carrying my drop looks under-resourced. Also, is it plus 18% GST? ₹1,999 plus GST is about ₹2,359. I'd sign up for the trial. I'd pay after the trial only if it handled a real capsule drop cleanly. What makes me upgrade: comment-to-DM, own WhatsApp number, seats, a drop pass. What makes me leave: one public embarrassment, an outage during a drop, a Meta flag on my account, or stock going out of sync with Shopify. Quiet months are a waste at ₹1,999 for 200 orders, so I'd ask for a pause or downgrade option between drops.

Flat vs per-order vs percent of sales: I trust a flat fee, because I can budget it and tell Kriti the number. A per-order fee is fine because ₹2 on a ₹1,300 order is nothing and I expect to pay for volume, as long as there is a cap or a pre-agreed drop pack so a viral weekend doesn't surprise me. Percentage of sales feels like an agency commission and I already have one of those.

**9. Free plan or free trial? How long? Annual discount?**

A free plan is not what I need. A trial is, but 15 days is the wrong shape for a drop business: my next drop might be 6 weeks away and I won't test on a real drop in 15 days. I'd want 30 days minimum, or a trial that runs until after one real capsule drop of about 200 units. A better offer: a paid pilot with a human on the line. Annual: yes, after one clean drop, and 2 months free (about 17%) is enough to tempt me. 20 to 25% would be nice, but price isn't what's stopping me.

**10. Add-ons you would actually pay for**

- Drop-day support: human on call, a rehearsal the week before, a post-drop report: about ₹15,000 per drop. I would pay this on day one.
- White-label and custom domain for links, order pages, tracking: about ₹2,000 a month, but honestly this should be in the plan for anyone with a brand.
- Auto-replies on my own WhatsApp number: ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 a month.
- Comment-to-DM: should be in Pro; if it's an add-on, ₹1,500 a month.
- Extra staff logins with roles: 5 included, then about ₹300 a seat a month.
- WhatsApp broadcast packs at Meta's rate plus at most about ₹0.15 a message. I'd buy 50,000 a drop if it's cheaper than Interakt.
- COD confirmation over WhatsApp: about ₹3 per COD order, which is about ₹1,800 a drop.
- Done-for-you setup, catalog upload and training the voice on my captions and FAQs, with QA of 100 test chats: ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 one time.
- Sponsor analytics pack, a branded PDF or shareable dashboard: ₹2,000 to ₹3,000 a month.
- Unlimited AI replies in drop week, since a cap that stops replies at hour one is worse than no tool.

**11. Who else influences the decision, and what they'd ask**

- **Kriti (manager):** runs every tool. Asks: "Does it sync with Shopify? Who do I call at 2 am during a drop? What's the exit if it breaks?"
- **The agency lead, Nikhil:** this touches his retainer, so he'll probably push back first. Asks: "What do we do with the store and Interakt we built? Who's accountable if it fails?"

**12. One sentence to a friend**

"It's a ManyChat-plus-order-desk that could finally fix my 'link?' comments, but until comment-to-DM and my own domain are live and someone shows me it survives a 1,500-order weekend, it's a test on a small capsule drop, not my store."
