# CartHedge should meter AI replies, not sales

CartHedge's list prices of ₹499, ₹999 and ₹1,999 sit on the price points Indian small sellers already recognise. Its flat, no-cut-of-sales model is also a real edge in a market where storefronts take 2–10% of GMV. The problem is that the lineup loses money exactly where the product is most differentiated. The AI sales assistant costs an estimated **₹1.35–2.49 in model inference for every DM-originated order**. Pro charges about ₹1 per included order (₹0.85 net if the price includes GST), so for a seller whose DMs the assistant runs, Pro sits at **−30% to −98% contribution margin**. Growth earns about 38%, and once support and maintenance are counted no plan reaches the ~70% SaaS gross-margin median, even at 1,000 sellers. The evidence supports keeping order-count tiers as the main meter, because sellers count orders and a flat per-order price never taxes basket size. On top of that, CartHedge should add a second allowance for AI replies, prepaid only, that pauses the assistant when used up rather than blocking orders. Shown ex-GST like every competitor, a **₹499 / ₹1,499 / ₹3,999 + GST** ladder with the full assistant in the badged middle tier earns roughly **52–68% gross margin even when sellers use their entire AI allowance**. That ladder still undercuts the WhatsApp-tool incumbents (Interakt Growth ₹2,799, AiSensy Pro ₹3,200, WATI Growth ₹4,999) and costs a fraction of a Shopify-plus-Razorpay stack. AI alone will not carry a price: Instagram DM automation has gone free, Meta's Business Agent is free to start, and sellers expect software to cost ₹300–1,000 a month. The pitch therefore has to rest on the order desk, the storefront and COD/RTO savings; one bounced ₹1,000 COD order costs a seller ₹200–250. A second revenue line can come from AI top-ups, transparent WhatsApp credits, payment-partner commissions, paid setup and a self-serve COD check, though most partner rates are unpublished. The biggest unknown is usage. There is no production data yet on AI turns per order or on the share of orders that arrive by DM, and a 2× error in either moves AI cost 2×.

## Storefronts charge ₹0–2,000 a month, then take 2–10% of every sale

Indian storefronts keep subscriptions cheap and make their money on GMV. **Shopify India's Basic plan costs ₹1,994 a month, or ₹1,499 billed annually** ([Shopify India pricing](https://www.shopify.com/in/pricing)). Shopify Payments does not operate in India, so every merchant also pays a **2% third-party transaction fee on Basic (1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced)**, on top of Razorpay's own 2% plus GST ([Razorpay blog](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-shopify-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/)). A ₹1,000 order on Basic therefore loses ₹43.60 before shipping ([CrawlApps](https://crawlapps.com/blogs/news/shopify-pricing-in-india)). Instamojo gives away a Lite store but charges **5% + ₹3 per transaction**, which falls to 2% + ₹3 on its ₹14,999-a-year Growth plan ([Instamojo pricing](https://www.instamojo.com/pricing/)). Dukaan's help centre lists a service fee of 4.99% on Free, 3.99% on Starter and 1.99% on Growth, capped at ₹200 an order and charged on top of gateway fees. That figure could only be read from a search snippet ([Dukaan help](https://help.mydukaan.io/article/1014-dukaan-service-fee-payment-gateway-charges)). SuperProfile takes 10% on its free plan and 5% on its ₹11,999-a-year Premium ([SuperProfile help](https://help.cosmofeed.com/portal/en/kb/articles/superprofile-plans-passion-pro)). Zoho Commerce is the exception: ₹99 a month, 0% transaction fee and a 14-day no-card trial ([Zoho Commerce](https://www.zoho.com/commerce/pricing.html)). But it is a catalogue builder, not a tool for selling in chat.

Most tools built for WhatsApp-first sellers have left the field. **Bikayi laid off about 90% of its staff over four months of 2022 while monthly revenue fell from about ₹1.9 crore to under ₹10 lakh.** A seller exodus and fraud allegations came with the fall, and Bikayi then pivoted to enterprise customers ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/bikayi-in-disarray-startup-hit-by-fraud-allegations-seller-exodus/)). DotPe now sells to offline businesses ([DotPe](https://dotpe.tech/)), and SuperProfile serves creators selling digital products. Meesho remains the free alternative: it charges 0% commission, but the seller pays shipping and absorbs ₹75–140 on each failed order ([Blooprint](https://blooprint.in/blog/meesho-seller-fees-explained/)). That leaves an open position for a tool aimed squarely at social sellers of physical goods.

Checkout and RTO tools are priced for brands far larger than CartHedge's sellers. GoKwik publishes no rate card. Its best fit is brands with **3,000+ orders a month**, COD above about 40% and AOV under about ₹1,500, and quotes in this category commonly take 0.5–1% of checkout GMV ([The Shizz](https://theshizz.in/blog/gokwik-vs-razorpay-magic-checkout-comparison-d2c)). Razorpay Magic Checkout is sold through sales on enterprise pricing ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)), and Shopflo reportedly starts at ₹3,000–5,000 a month ([WiserReview](https://wiserreview.com/blog/shopflo-alternatives/)). The only published per-order RTO price found is **Shiprocket's RTO Score at ₹2.49 per COD order** ([Shiprocket support](https://support.shiprocket.in/support/solutions/articles/152000000313-how-much-will-rto-score-cost-me-)). The pay-later checkout option Simpl was ordered by the RBI to stop payment operations in September 2025 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/rbi-orders-bnpl-startup-simpl-to-halt-payment-operations-over-regulatory-breach-495875-2025-09-26)). Between 100 and 500 orders a month, CartHedge faces no direct checkout or COD-risk competitor. Only around 2,000 orders a month does it start to meet the bottom of GoKwik's and Shopflo's range.

Shipping aggregators also earn on usage rather than on subscriptions. Shiprocket's plans run from free to ₹799 a month, with average freight falling from ₹45 to ₹36 per shipment, and the plan fee is refunded once a seller reaches 100, 500 or 1,000 shipments ([Shiprocket pricing](https://www.shiprocket.in/pricing/)). NimbusPost charges no subscription and quotes ₹19–25.50 per 500 g ([NimbusPost](https://nimbuspost.com/pricing)). Going direct to Delhivery costs ₹55–70 per shipment at small volume ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/delhivery-courier-charges)). Shiprocket's COD fee is **₹27 + 1.5% with a ₹37 minimum** ([CheckThat](https://checkthat.ai/brands/shiprocket/pricing)), so collecting a single COD order costs a seller more than CartHedge charges per order on any plan.

Two conclusions follow. First, a flat price with no cut of GMV saves a Shopify Basic seller about **₹800, ₹4,000 and ₹16,000 a month** at these three volumes, which makes "no cut of your sales" CartHedge's sharpest message. Second, logistics and COD fees are larger than software at every volume. The claim most likely to justify a higher CartHedge price is therefore fewer COD orders and fewer RTOs, not cheaper software.

## Chat tools sell AI as a metered extra while Instagram DMs go free

Indian WhatsApp and Instagram inbox tools fall into three price bands. The entry band is **₹999–1,500 a month**: BotSpace Pro starts at ₹999 for 500 engaged contacts and includes an AI agent ([BotSpace](https://www.bot.space/pricing)), Interakt's ₹999 Starter is Instagram-only on monthly billing ([CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/interakt-pricing-india-2026)), and AiSensy Basic is ₹1,500 ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/aisensy-price-hike-may-2026-alternatives-india)). The growth band is ₹2,500–3,500, where AiSensy Pro rose about 19% to ₹3,200 in May 2026 (same source). The team band starts at ₹5,000: WATI Growth costs ₹4,999 with a hard cap of three users ([WATI pricing](https://www.wati.io/pricing/)), and Gallabox reserves AI agents for its ₹6,999 Essential plan ([Gallabox pricing](https://gallabox.com/pricing)). Annual discounts cluster at 10–20%. AI-first platforms aimed at D2C brands, such as Bik and LimeChat, are sold through sales teams and publish no prices.

AI is priced in three ways: credits bundled into the plan (WATI, Gallabox), a flat add-on that works out to about **₹1.35 per AI message** (AiSensy), and per outcome, at about ₹8.7–10 per resolution or, for Zoko's sales agent, **3% of order value** ([Zoko](https://www.zoko.io/pricing)). Zoko's sales agent is the closest analogue to CartHedge's assistant, and it shows that GMV-linked AI pricing has a precedent. Meta is now the ceiling-setter. Its Business Agent answers questions, recommends catalogue products and closes sales across WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger. Meta says "getting started is free", says paid subscriptions will follow "in the coming months", and reports more than one million businesses already using it ([WhatsApp Business blog](https://whatsappbusiness.com/blog/introducing-meta-business-agent-ai/)). On the API, Meta prices the agent at about **4–5 US cents (roughly ₹4–5) per message** from 1 August 2026 ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages)). In practice, one AI reply sells for ₹1.35–5 across this market, while CartHedge's estimated cost is ₹0.11–0.30 per turn (see below). There is room to charge for AI. What does not hold up is charging for basic AI answers when Meta gives them away.

Messaging costs moved twice in 2026. India's WhatsApp marketing rate rose about 10% to **₹0.8631 per message** on 1 January, and utility and authentication messages stay at about ₹0.115, before 18% GST ([Chat Mitra](https://chatmitra.com/blog/whatsapp-business-cost-per-message/)). From **1 October 2026, Meta charges service replies inside the 24-hour window at the utility rate**. Several partners say the first 1,000 such replies per number per month stay free ([360dialog](https://360dialog.com/blog/whatsapp-service-message-charging-october-2026/); [AiSensy](https://aisensy.com/pricing)), but Meta's own non-template pricing page reads "Free Messages: None after October 1, 2026" ([Meta developer docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages)). This conflict is unresolved. Instagram messaging carries no per-message fee ([go-live notes](../../WHATSAPP-INSTAGRAM-GO-LIVE.md)), so AI replies on Instagram are structurally cheaper than on WhatsApp. Indian WhatsApp tools typically mark up Meta's rates by 10–26% on marketing messages and up to about 40% on utility messages. BotSpace and Zoko advertise passing them through at cost.

Two complaints recur. The first is real bills far above the headline price: WATI customers report totals of about five times the plan price once Meta fees and add-ons are counted, and also report that cancelling is hard ([BossBot](https://bossbot.uk/blog/wati-pricing-review-2026)). The second is price rises without notice: AiSensy's May 2026 increase drew a wave of competitor "switch" content ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/aisensy-price-hike-may-2026-alternatives-india)). Instagram automation, meanwhile, is racing to free. Zorcha made core DM automation free in August 2026, ManyChat cut its free tier from 1,000 contacts to 25 in March 2026, and ReplyKaro sells for ₹99 a month ([UnlockDM](https://www.tryunlockdm.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-india-2026)). CartHedge cannot charge for comment-to-DM automation or for basic replies. But no vendor in this set bundles a storefront and an order desk with an AI sales agent at ₹999–1,500, and that bundle is the position CartHedge can own.

## Sellers pay ₹300–1,000 a month and lose ₹200–250 per failed COD order

CartHedge's segment is smaller and faster-growing than the headline "social commerce" figures suggest. Redseer sizes digitally enabled social commerce, meaning offline-first MSMEs that sell through chat and need help with enablement or logistics, at **US$1.4–2.2 billion of GMV in 2024, growing 40–50% a year to 2029**. In these businesses orders arrive by DM, buyers pay through UPI apps and confirm with screenshots, and they sit within India's 55–60 million retail MSMEs ([Redseer for Shiprocket](https://sr-website-01.shiprocket.in/sr-website/IndustryReport-MhCbDH.pdf)). Older forecasts of $16–20 billion include reseller platforms such as Meesho and should not be used to size this segment. Growth is concentrated outside the metros: 60% of new online sellers since 2021 came from Tier-2 or smaller cities ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-india-shops-online-2025/)), and Tier-2/3 cities drove 66% of incremental D2C order volume in FY2026 ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)). No credible count of Instagram- or WhatsApp-first sellers exists.

COD economics are the core of the seller's pain. Prepaid orders passed 50% only at the 2025 festive peak (52.42%, up from 37.44% a year earlier), and COD orders return to origin at several times the prepaid rate: **roughly 20–26% for COD against 2–10% for prepaid** over FY23–FY25 ([Angadi Labs](https://www.angadilabs.com/blog/india-fashion-ecommerce-benchmarks)). During the November 2025 festive quarter, overall RTO reached 39.2% and COD orders returned at 58%. What separated brands at 39% from those at 21% was prepaid incentives, courier routing by pin code and address verification at checkout ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)). GoKwik estimates that a bounced ₹1,000 COD order costs **₹200–250** once return freight and processing are counted ([Angadi Labs](https://www.angadilabs.com/blog/india-fashion-ecommerce-benchmarks)). For a 500-order seller with 50% COD, cutting COD RTO by five points avoids about 12 RTOs, worth roughly ₹2,800 a month, which is close to twice the ₹1,499 Growth price proposed below. All of this data comes from D2C brands. DM-first micro-sellers probably sit at the worse end of these figures, but no data on them was found.

Willingness to pay is anchored low and shaped by how sellers pay. The software a micro-business already pays for is billing software at about ₹291–333 a month billed annually. myBillBook charges ₹3,490–3,990 a year and includes a seat for the seller's CA and a seven-day money-back guarantee ([myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/pricing-plans)). Zoho Books is free below ₹25 lakh turnover ([Zoho Books](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/pricing/pricing-comparison.html)). Bikayi is the cautionary case. It raised its annual subscription from ₹5,000 to ₹30,000 between 2021 and 2022, and **up to 90% of buyers chose the EMI option at ₹18,000**. When EMI was withdrawn, monthly revenue fell from ₹1.9 crore to ₹9.5 lakh, and Inc42 reported that even the ₹5,000 plan made money only if sellers also bought marketing-credit add-ons ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/bikayi-in-disarray-startup-hit-by-fraud-allegations-seller-exodus/)). Dukaan shows both sides of charging. Dropping its free plan coincided with crossing $1.7 million ARR in 2022 ([The Investment Thesis](https://srijithpadmesh.substack.com/p/dukaan)), but adding an unannounced per-order fee in October 2023 drew a public backlash on X ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/entrepreneurship/start-up/story/e-commerce-platform-dukaan-introduces-service-fee-receives-backlash-on-x-founder-responds-402341-2023-10-17)). Monthly payment through UPI AutoPay is the modern equivalent of EMI. Mandates are now portable across UPI apps ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/npci-set-to-make-upi-autopay-mandates-portable-across-apps-report-12458130)), but about 20 million are revoked every month for low balances ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/upi-autopay-revocations-hit-20-mn-monthly-over-low-customer-balances-125090700500_1.html)), so retries and a grace period are essential. No survey with quantitative willingness-to-pay, trial-conversion or churn data for Indian micro-sellers was found. A practitioner's view that ₹999 a month is the "sweet spot" for Indian micro-SaaS is opinion, not data ([Rajesh Nair](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/software/saas/micro-saas-ideas-india)).

The seller voices that could be captured point the same way. Indian merchants reviewing a Shopify COD-confirmation app credit it with fewer fake orders, cancellations and RTOs, and one names Meta ads as a source of fraudulent COD orders. Another reports ten days of support silence, which is the kind of failure that triggers churn ([Shopify App Store reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/cod-order-confirmation-1/reviews)). Reddit, Quora and Play Store reviews were not reachable. The widely repeated story of sellers spending hours answering "price?" in DMs therefore rests on vendor guides ([StitchMagic](https://stitchmagic.in/guides/sell-on-instagram-india)) and still needs first-hand interviews to confirm.

Buyers are moving to UPI but still use COD as a way to manage trust. Gen Z makes up 40% of e-retail shoppers, and more than 90% of them prefer UPI ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-india-shops-online-2025/)). Instagram shop scams teach buyers caution: cloned seller handles, prices far below market, and phone numbers that go dead after payment ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fraud-alert-social-media-eshop-scams/76634.html)). A verifiable storefront with an order page and tracking link therefore does real work in converting COD buyers to prepaid. Buyers are also saturated with WhatsApp messages. In a survey of 42,000+ people, **96% said they get spam or promotional WhatsApp messages daily and 59% said they block senders**, and most of that spam comes through WhatsApp Business ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/social-media/whatsapp-spam-hits-96-of-users-in-india-reveals-survey-amid-sc-heat-on-meta-88863.htm)). In a separate survey, 75% of online shoppers said they often meet drip pricing, including undisclosed COD fees ([BW Businessworld](https://www.businessworld.in/article/localcircles-says-75-indian-ecom-users-face-drip-pricing-575259)). So consent-gated broadcasts and COD charges shown upfront are selling points, not limitations. No survey was found on how large a prepaid discount it takes to move a buyer off COD.

## An AI-run order costs CartHedge more than Pro charges for it

The cost model below combines current vendor prices with token and message counts estimated from CartHedge's code. All derivations are in the [cost-to-serve model](../research_notes/CartHedge%20pricing%20market%20research/cost_to_serve_model.md), and it uses ₹96.3 to the dollar ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/india/currency)).

**Messaging.** In phase 1, CartHedge sends WhatsApp messages from its own number, about 3.5 billable messages per order on a 50/50 mix of storefront and Instagram orders. At the ₹0.115 utility rate that is **≈₹0.40 per order**. This is cheap per order but grows in a straight line: at 2,000 orders it reaches ₹805, about half of Pro's net revenue. In phase 2, sellers use their own WhatsApp number, and each AI reply becomes a billable service message once any free allowance is used up. YCloud adds no markup on top of Meta's rate ([YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/tech-partner)). Gupshup reportedly adds about $0.001 on every message, incoming ones included ([CodingClave](https://codingclave.com/blog/gupshup-whatsapp-pricing-india-2026)), which would push a 500-order seller's WhatsApp cost to ₹1,178 against ₹446 on YCloud and wipe out Growth's margin. CartHedge's WhatsApp account must also stay Indian-registered: an OTP sent from a foreign account to an Indian number costs about ₹2.50 instead of ₹0.115 ([Monty Mobile](https://montymobile.com/blogs/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-in-india-inr-rates-gst-and-the-2026-currency-migration-deadline/)).

**AI.** Each assistant turn makes two calls. The first ("understand") carries up to 80 catalogue lines and the last 30 messages; the second ("write") composes the reply. Together they come to an estimated **6,200 input and 380 output tokens per turn**. At OpenAI's list prices of $0.40 input, $0.10 cached input and $1.60 output per million tokens for gpt-4.1-mini, and $0.10 / $0.025 / $0.40 for gpt-4.1-nano ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)), a turn costs about ₹0.21 on mini with half the input cached. That is two to three times the repo's earlier assumption of "well under ₹0.10". A converting chat runs about 7–8 turns, and each order also carries about two short browsing chats that never convert, so the base case is **about 12 turns per DM order**, with a plausible range of 8–18.

| Model, 50% of input cached | Cost per turn | Cost per DM order (12 turns) |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-4.1-mini | ₹0.208 | ₹2.49 |
| Hybrid: nano "understand" + mini "write" | ₹0.112 | ₹1.35 |
| gpt-4.1-nano | ₹0.052 | ₹0.62 |
| Gemini 3.6 Flash (current failover), 2026 | ₹0.384 | ≈₹4.61 |
| Gemini 3.6 Flash, from 1 Jan 2027 | ₹0.77 | ≈₹9.24 |

The failover row is a hidden risk. CartHedge's configured fallback, `gemini-3.6-flash` ([integration notes](../../INTEGRATIONS.md)), costs $0.75 / $3.75 per million tokens until 31 December 2026, and that price doubles on 1 January 2027 ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). At that point a failover day costs 3.7 times a normal day. The admin AI-spend view also under-reports cost: it converts at ₹85 to the dollar and prices every Gemini model at Flash-Lite rates. Cache hits are also lower than they could be. The prompt places per-turn data after the catalogue, so only about 40% of each turn's input forms a reusable prefix.

**Infrastructure** is small and mostly fixed. Render's web service costs $7–25 a month ([srvrlss.io](https://www.srvrlss.io/provider/render/)). Neon's Launch compute costs $0.106 per CU-hour ([Neon](https://neon.com/pricing)) and will run around the clock, because the server's five-minute job sweeps keep the database awake. Cloudflare R2 storage ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)) and Resend email ([Resend](https://resend.com/pricing)) cost little per seller. SMS fallback through MSG91 costs ₹0.16–0.25 per message ([MSG91](https://msg91.com/in/pricing/sms)), which is 1.4–2.2 times a WhatsApp authentication message, so WhatsApp should stay the primary channel. Taken together, infrastructure comes to about **₹7,700, ₹14,600 and ₹31,100 a month at 50, 300 and 1,000 sellers**, or ₹154, ₹49 and ₹31 per seller.

**Payments and tax.** Collecting a subscription through Razorpay costs 2% plus GST on the fee, or 2.36% in total, on any domestic method including UPI. Recurring card charges add 0.9%, and UPI AutoPay pricing is available only on request ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)). GST is the bigger swing. SaaS is taxed at 18% ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/gst-on-software-services)), so a GST-inclusive ₹999 nets only **₹846.61**. Meta, OpenAI, Google, Render and Neon are all imported services taxed under reverse charge. If CartHedge is GST-registered it reclaims that 18%; if it is not, every dollar-billed line in this model costs 18% more.

**People** are the largest fixed cost. A customer-care executive in India earns about ₹2.57 lakh a year ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/IN/Job=Customer_Care_Executive/Salary)), and a software engineer with 1–4 years' experience about ₹7.5 lakh ([PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/IN/Job=Software_Engineer/Salary)). The model assumes ₹30,000 a month fully loaded for a support executive and ₹1 lakh a month for a developer who can own the Go, Postgres and Meta integrations. B2B SaaS benchmarks run 0.2–0.5 support tickets per customer per month ([IdeaPlan](https://www.ideaplan.io/metrics/support-ticket-volume)). Non-technical sellers are assumed at 1–2 tickets, or about ₹43–86 per seller per month at ₹43 a ticket, and Zoho Desk's free tier covers up to three agents ([Zoho Desk INR pricing](https://www.zoho.com/desk/images/desk-pricing-inr.pdf)). With a 0.25–1 FTE maintenance share included, fixed cost per paying seller falls from **≈₹654 at 50 sellers to ≈₹315 at 300 and ≈₹194 at 1,000**, and people account for about ₹162 of that ₹194.

The table puts these together per seller per month, assuming the assistant handles the 50% of orders that arrive by DM:

Today the assistant is Pro-only ([product spec](../../../product.txt)). Starter has no AI, and Growth uses one-shot draft parsing, which costs an estimated ₹300 a month. With that packaging, the plans perform as follows:

The benchmarks are a median gross margin of 71–74% for private SaaS companies ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-gross-margin-benchmarks-2026)) and 55–70% for compute-heavy AI products ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin/)). There is one fair objection to the table: "fully loaded" counts the maintenance developer as a cost of serving sellers, whereas conventional SaaS accounting puts that cost in R&D. Removing it raises Starter to roughly 53–66% and Growth to 20–27%, but Pro stays between −36% and −39%. **Pro's problem is variable cost, not overhead**, and on gpt-4.1-mini throughout it is twice as bad. Starter and Growth are squeezed by people costs, which scale reduces. Pro is squeezed by AI, which grows with every DM it answers.

## Two meters and a ₹499–₹3,999 ladder lift margins to 52–68%

The evidence rules out three pricing models. A percentage of GMV is what incumbents charge, it is what sellers resented when Dukaan introduced it, and it would blunt CartHedge's sharpest message. Buyer money also settles in the seller's own Razorpay account, so CartHedge could not collect such a fee in any case. Per-seat pricing reads as nickel-and-diming in a market where AiSensy advertises unlimited users ([AiSensy](https://aisensy.com/pricing)). Per-outcome AI pricing such as Zoko's 3% of order value brings a sales-linked meter back in by another route. What the evidence supports is **order-count tiers as the primary meter**. Sellers already accept volume-linked pricing: GoKwik's Shopify app sets its tiers by monthly order count ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/kwikcheckout)), and Shiprocket refunds its plan fee at volume thresholds. Order count is the unit a seller already tracks, and it never grows with basket size.

Orders, however, are the wrong unit of cost. AI cost follows conversations, including browsing chats that never become orders, so a seller with a low chat-to-order conversion rate costs twice as much to serve as one with a high rate, and an order meter cannot see the difference. The fix is a second allowance for AI replies inside each plan. The obvious objection is that a separately metered AI add-on, as AiSensy sells it, compounds the bill-shock risk that sellers complain about. The design below avoids that by making the AI allowance **prepaid only**. Replies come included in the plan, optional top-up packs are bought in advance, and there is never a post-paid AI overage. When replies run out, the assistant drops back to drafting orders and hands chats to the seller, and orders are never blocked. The guardrails come from the same evidence. Alerts go out over WhatsApp at 80% and 100% of each allowance. Order overage stops accruing once the month's bill reaches the price of the next tier, and the seller is offered the upgrade at that point.

| Plan | Monthly price, ex-GST (incl. 18% GST) | Annual, two months free | Orders included | Per extra order | AI replies included | What the plan adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lite (after trial; experiment) | ₹0 | — | 20 | Upgrade to continue | 0 | Storefront, order links, order board, CartHedge badge; notifications by Instagram chat only |
| Starter | ₹499 (₹589) | ₹4,990 | 100 | ₹3 | 300 | COD confirmation, WhatsApp status updates, customer ledger, a taster of the assistant |
| **Growth (most popular)** | **₹1,499 (₹1,769)** | **₹14,990** | **500** | **₹2.50** | **2,500** | Full AI sales assistant, consent-gated broadcasts, offers and reseller pricing, GST invoices, dropped-checkout reminders |
| Pro | ₹3,999 (₹4,719) | ₹39,990 | 2,000 | ₹2 | 8,000 | Courier handoff, back-in-stock waitlists, staff roles, priority support |
| AI top-up | ₹399 per 1,000 replies | — | — | — | +1,000 | Prepaid, never billed after the fact |

Each price point has an anchor in the market. **Growth carries the full assistant and the "most popular" badge**, which follows Instamojo's practice of making its middle tier the obvious choice ([Instamojo](https://www.instamojo.com/pricing/)). At ₹1,499 it matches AiSensy Basic and Shopify Basic's annual price, and it sits well below AiSensy Basic plus its AI add-on (₹2,850) and Interakt Growth (₹2,799), both of which charge message fees on top. Growth's annual price of ₹14,990 lands on Instamojo Growth's ₹14,999, and the monthly price works out to about ₹50 a day, the same daily framing Instamojo uses. Starter keeps the familiar ₹499 entry price but now includes a 300-reply taster, because an entry plan without AI is competing against Meta's free agent and free Instagram tools. Pro doubles to ₹3,999 but still comes to ₹2 per order. That is below WATI Growth (₹4,999) and Gallabox's cheapest plan with AI agents (₹6,999), and about a quarter of what Shopify Basic's 2% fee alone would take on ₹16 lakh of GMV. The per-order overage rates stay where they are: once AI is metered separately, a ₹2–3 overage is five to seven times the ₹0.40 WhatsApp cost of an order. At ₹0.40 a reply, the AI top-up earns about 3.6 times its hybrid cost, while costing a third of AiSensy's per-message price and about a tenth of Meta's agent on the API.

The margins below assume 300 sellers and that every seller uses its full order quota and its whole AI allowance, so they are the worst case within each plan. The cost basis is phase-1 WhatsApp, Razorpay, infrastructure (₹49) and ticket-driven support (₹43, ₹65 and ₹86 per seller):

| Plan | Price ex-GST | WhatsApp + AI + Razorpay (hybrid AI) | Infrastructure + support | Gross margin, hybrid AI | Gross margin, all-nano AI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | ₹499 | ₹86 | ₹92 | 64% | 68% |
| Growth | ₹1,499 | ₹517 | ₹114 | 58% | 68% |
| Pro | ₹3,999 | ₹1,796 | ₹135 | 52% | 64% |
| Today's Pro, same basis (₹1,999 incl. GST) | ₹1,694 | ₹2,202 | ₹135 | −38% | — |

If sellers use half their AI allowance, margins rise to about 63–68% on the hybrid setup, before any revenue from top-ups or overage. Adding the cost model's maintenance-developer share of about ₹167 per seller at 300 sellers lowers the worst-case figures to about 31%, 47% and 48%; at 1,000 sellers, where that share falls to ₹100, they become 47%, 52% and 50%. Starter is the plan most exposed to fixed people costs, so support deflection through in-app guides and self-serve help protects it more than any change to its price. Cost fixes do as much for Pro as the price rise does. Running the "understand" call on a nano-class model, once it passes the repo's Hinglish evaluation suite (the [production notes](../../PRODUCTION.md) warn that small models struggle with Hinglish catalogue matching), lifts Pro from 52% to 64%. Reordering the prompt so the instructions and catalogue form one cacheable prefix, and switching the failover model to Flash-Lite before January 2027, protect that gain. Sending order notifications through Instagram chat and web push wherever possible then cuts into Pro's largest remaining line, the ₹805 of WhatsApp messages.

Presentation and migration choices matter as much as the price points. **Every comparable tool quotes prices before GST**: Zoho shows them "exclusive of GST", Interakt says taxes are extra, AiSensy adds "+ GST", and Shopify excludes it ([Zoho Books](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/pricing/pricing-comparison.html); [CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/interakt-pricing-india-2026)). If CartHedge's current prices include GST, switching to "+ 18% GST" alone raises net revenue by 18%. Because many sellers are unregistered and cannot reclaim the tax, the pricing page should show the GST-inclusive figure next to the base price, so there is no surprise at checkout. "Two months free" on annual plans, about 17% off, matches WATI and Zoho Books and sits below Gallabox (20%) and Shopify (25%). Monthly payment through UPI AutoPay should remain the default, with retries, given the Bikayi experience.

The 15-day no-card trial is in line with the market: 62% of B2B products use 14-day trials, no-card trials convert at 4–6% (good) to 10–15% (great), and AI products convert slightly better ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)). The Lite tier is a reverse-trial test rather than a commitment. Free tiers are standard among Instagram tools, sellers with seasonal demand keep their storefront link alive, and a Lite seller costs only a share of infrastructure, roughly ₹30–50 a month, with no WhatsApp spend. Lite should be dropped if it converts to paid below the no-card trial benchmark or if its support load grows.

Existing sellers should keep their current price for a fixed window, such as six months, and get at least 30 days' notice of the change, because the Dukaan and AiSensy episodes show that unannounced rises turn into public poaching. A "founder price for life" is unsafe on Pro, which loses money on every seller whose DMs the AI handles.

## Payment referrals, WhatsApp credits and COD checks form a second revenue line

CartHedge's architecture suits payment referrals. Buyer money already settles in each seller's own Razorpay account, so onboarding sellers through Razorpay's Technology Partner programme earns a "fixed partner commission" on their transactions at no cost to the seller. Razorpay does not publish the rate ([Razorpay partners](https://razorpay.com/partners/)). Third parties report about 0.1% plus a referral bonus of up to ₹500 ([Xamplify](https://xamplify.com/partner-programs/razorpay/)), and Cashfree's affiliate programme reports 0.25–0.35% ([Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/affiliate-partner-program-industry-best-commission/)), a figure that still needs checking against zero-MDR rules for UPI. At 0.1% of prepaid GMV, a 500-order seller would earn CartHedge about ₹200 a month and a 2,000-order seller about ₹800, a meaningful amount next to the plan fee. A referral arrangement also avoids the payment-aggregator licence that the spec's "payment take-rate (later)" idea would require ([product spec](../../../product.txt)).

The other streams vary in how solid their evidence is:

| Stream | Market anchor | Indicative CartHedge economics | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI reply top-ups | AiSensy ≈₹1.35 per AI message; Meta's agent ≈₹4–5 per message | ₹0.40 per reply, ≈70% margin | High: the cost is known |
| WhatsApp credits (phase 2) | BSP markups of 10–30%; BotSpace and Zoko at cost | Meta rate plus a visible fee; the repo plans ≈₹1.25 per offer message | Medium |
| Payment referral | Razorpay fixed commission (unpublished); 0.1% (third-party); Cashfree 0.25–0.35% | ≈₹200–800 per seller per month at 500–2,000 orders | Low until a rate is negotiated |
| Done-for-you setup | Instamojo's "Do-It-For-Me" service, priced on request ([Instamojo](https://www.instamojo.com/pricing/)) | ₹999–2,999 one-off for catalogue import, storefront setup and AI tone training | Low: untested |
| Verified COD / RTO risk score | Shiprocket RTO Score at ₹2.49 per COD order | ₹1–3 per checked COD order, or bundled into Pro | Medium |
| Courier margin | Aggregator margin of 5–12% ([CheckThat](https://checkthat.ai/brands/shiprocket/pricing)); Shiprocket affiliate payouts unpublished ([Shiprocket partners](https://www.shiprocket.in/partners/)) | ₹3–8 per shipment, per the product plan | Low |

WhatsApp credits are the industry's main margin lever, but they become available only once sellers run their own numbers through a partner in phase 2. They should be sold the way the complaints demand: Meta's rate and CartHedge's fee shown side by side, a prepaid wallet with a low minimum (AiSensy's is ₹10), and no non-refundable balances of the kind BotSpace keeps ([BotSpace](https://www.bot.space/pricing)). Paid setup turns the "we'll import your catalog for you on the call" promise in the [production notes](../../PRODUCTION.md) from an acquisition cost into revenue. It can also be waived on annual plans as an incentive to commit. COD verification is the add-on with the clearest value, since every avoided RTO saves ₹200–250. But basic COD confirmation should stay in Starter, because it is the core of the value story. The paid layer is risk scoring, and later the RTO-protection premium the product plan describes. Razorpay Magic Checkout already offers prepaid nudges, COD fees by order value and COD blocking for high-risk buyers, but only to enterprise clients ([Razorpay Magic](https://razorpay.com/magic/)), which leaves room for a self-serve version at small-seller prices. Extra seats are a weak lever and lending referrals have no published Indian economics, so both should wait.

## Conclusion

The central finding is a mismatch between what CartHedge meters and what drives its costs. The order is the right unit of value: sellers count orders, competitors' GMV percentages make a flat per-order price look generous, and RTO savings are measured per order. But AI cost follows conversations, and Meta's October 2026 billing change means WhatsApp cost follows them too. Splitting the two meters, with a cheap order allowance and a prepaid reply allowance, lets CartHedge keep "no cut of your sales" while closing the losses on Pro. CartHedge's price is squeezed from both sides. Meta and the Instagram tools set a price of zero for basic AI answers, and CartHedge's own inference cost sets a floor that a ₹1-per-order plan sits below. The defensible ground in between is outcomes Meta's agent does not deliver: orders captured, COD buyers confirmed, RTOs avoided.

Three things will settle the remaining uncertainty and should be tracked before prices are finalised. The first is two to four weeks of production AI-spend data on turns per order and DM share, which will resize the allowances. The second is whether Meta's 1,000 free service messages survived October 2026, which changes phase-2 cost by about ₹115 per seller per month. The third is the price of Meta's promised paid Business Agent subscription, which will become the ceiling every AI DM tool in India is measured against. Until then, new prices should apply to new sellers first, with existing sellers on a fixed grandfathering window, so that real conversion data rather than estimates decides the final numbers.
